I'm Jeff Geiser — VP, Customer Engineering at [Zenlayer](https://zenlayer.com),
building in public at the intersection of enterprise AI and sovereign inference.
Based in Northern Virginia.

Day job: helping large companies deploy training and inference infrastructure,
focused mostly on edge compute. Side project turned main bet:
[Wicklee](https://wicklee.dev), hardware-first observability and cost
governance for self-hosted inference — the MPG rating for local AI. It
measures what your own GPUs actually cost, down to $/1M tokens, instead of
asking you to trust a vendor's number.

The thread underneath both: inference infrastructure people run themselves
deserves the same rigor cloud infrastructure has had for a decade — you
should be able to see what it costs, where it's running, and why. Wicklee is
the observability piece. Increasingly I'm also writing about the layer above
it: how a distributed system — one agent, or a whole network — decides where
inference should run, and what it would take to name that as its own
control-plane problem. That's [in the writing](/posts/), framed as
architecture thinking, not a product I'm building or claiming to own.

On the side I still run a small fine-tuning experiment — a 7B enterprise
model, personal research, not a product.

When I'm not in front of a screen I'm running — base building right now,
no race on the calendar yet.
